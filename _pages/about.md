I am a 6th-year Ph.D. candidate in Management (Organization Theory) at [Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/post-grad/people/yixi-chen). I will be on the 2026-2027 job market. 

My research sits at the intersection of **identity**, **social** **evaluation**, **entrepreneurship**, and **social** **networks**. I study how entrepreneurs and workers strategically present their stigmatized and marginalized identities, and how these choices shape investor evaluation, network formation, and well-being.

My dissertation examines a central puzzle in identity and social evaluation: when can a founder's stigmatized
identity become an unexpected source of advantage rather than remaining a liability? Across studies of
entrepreneurs with neurodivergent conditions and entrepreneurs with incarceration records, I develop and test the
concept of *stigma as distinction*, showing how stigmatized identities can become signals of distinctiveness in
contexts that reward differentiation. Using a combination of video and written-vignette experiments, surveys, and
interviews, I examine how stigma disclosure shapes investor evaluations and resource acquisition. My dissertation has been selected as a **finalist of the 2026 INFORMS/Organization Science Best Dissertation Proposal Competition**.

Before coming to Columbia, I worked as a Senior Analyst at Mastercard International (New York and Shanghai). I received my M.A. in Sociology from the University of Chicago and my B.A. in History and Economics from Mount Holyoke College.
